Somebody opened the door of your building before you arrived this morning.

Somebody checked the spreadsheet before it went into the deck. Somebody reviewed the calculation, the contract, the dosage. Somebody solved a problem at seven in the morning that never reached you — because it was solved.

Here is the mechanism, and it is the same at every level of the org chart: the more a piece of work prevents a problem, the less visible it becomes.

Nobody sees the fire that did not happen.

In 1513 Machiavelli set two men side by side. Sforza built his own foundation and kept his state with little trouble. Borgia inherited someone else's and lost everything the moment it moved out from under him. And the line that closes the chapter is the one for anyone who runs anything:

"He who has not first laid his foundations may be able to lay them afterwards, but with trouble to the architect and danger to the building."

Any organisation running for years on work it never measured is standing on a foundation it did not lay. While those people stay, the structure looks solid — and management credits the solidity to its own decisions.

The bill arrives when they leave.

Three things, and none of them cost money:

Measure what sustains, not only what interrupts.
Ask who taught them, before filing the process under the name of whoever wrote it down.
And check your own n = 0 — last time someone needed you, did you show up?
