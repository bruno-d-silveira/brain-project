A friend of mine died young. In his last days he drank through a straw. On a napkin he wrote what he missed doing most.

Not travelling. Not finishing anything.

Drinking a glass of water. Normally. Feeling it go down.

The hospital had water. The plumbing worked. The utility delivered everything it had promised. And still, for that man, in that room, there was no glass of water anywhere in the world.

Availability is not access. The figure balances, and the thirst continues.

I followed that distance upstream, across three scales — a state on the Nile, a basin with no border in the American desert, and private capital raising a 10-gigawatt campus on federal ground in Ohio.

The pattern does not change. The works go up. The rule arrives afterwards, if it arrives.

And in every case the failure was never the missing document. At Samos there was no document and it worked. On the Colorado there was one, and it tied itself to a river flow measured in the wettest decade of the century. At the UN a document was requested in June, and it was born with no binding force at all.

The variable is always the measurement underneath the paper.
